Its marker sits on 6, not 9 — Eminence is not counted, but the commander's own cost reduction is. Hover the marker to see why.

Why bars stacked by ROLE and not by colour or card type. A plain count histogram — what EDHREC, Deckstats and Moxfield draw — tells you the shape but not the problem. A 3-drop Rhystic Study and a 3-drop vanilla beater are the same bar, and they are not the same card. The EDH-specific diagnosis is role imbalance: nothing to do on turn two, or no cheap interaction, or ramp that stops at four. Colour belongs to the manabase question (pips and sources), not here; type is a leaky proxy for role, since Arcane Signet, Llanowar Elves and Cultivate are an artifact, a creature and a sorcery but one thing to a deckbuilder. By type is still a toggle, because that is what Archidekt and Goldfish show.

Two places I would deliberately break the rules, and you should veto if you disagree.

1. Split cards bucket at the cheaper half. The rules say Fire // Ice has mana value 4 in your library. But you are reading this chart to answer "what can I cast on turn two", and the answer for that card is yes. Rules-correct here would be practically wrong. It gets a // badge so it never lies silently.

2. Ramp is on the curve, not beside it. ManaBox splits "mana curve" from "mana production". I would keep ramp in the stack, because the question "is turn two empty" cannot be answered by a chart that hides your two-mana rocks. The legend still totals it separately.

And one number I am NOT putting on this screen: a grade. Two decks can both average 3.2 and play nothing alike — one smooth, one a pile of one-drops and nine-drops. Average MV is a small stat with a context band, never a score out of ten.

Assumes you hit your land drops and that ramp arrives on curve. It is a model, not a promise — the label says so, and the "Lands only" toggle shows the unramped floor so the optimistic number is never the only one you see.

This is the part the other tools do not have, and the part I am least sure about. Every Commander source says the same thing: a raw curve under-describes an EDH deck because ramp moves everything left. With twelve rocks a four-drop is a turn-three play. Moxfield ships a single number for the idea ("81.9% chance of playing these on curve"); a per-turn count is more legible but it is still a simulation with assumptions baked in.

My recommendation: build screen 1 first, and treat this as a second commit you can kill if it turns out you never look at it. Screen 1 stands alone. This does not.
