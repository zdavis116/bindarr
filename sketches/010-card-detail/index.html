Three ways to organise the same screen. All of them separate what the card is from what you own from what you can do — the current screen interleaves those, which is why the flip control had nowhere obvious to sit. Every variant makes flipping a full-width labelled control rather than an icon chip on the art.

A — one scroll, grouped. Everything on one column in three labelled groups. The flip control sits directly under the art, full width, naming the face you turn to. Both faces' rules text is shown at once, separated by a rule — you never have to flip just to read the back. Best if you mostly want to look things up.

C — faces as a switch. The two faces are a segmented control at the very top, so the whole screen (art, type line, rules) belongs to the face you picked. Closest to how Scryfall and Moxfield behave. Best if you think of a DFC as genuinely two cards. Cost: the back's rules text needs a tap.

My recommendation is A. It fixes the actual complaint — the flip control is a full-width labelled button that cannot be missed — and it shows both faces' rules at once, which is what you usually want when checking whether a card does what you remember. B and C are both shorter, but each puts something behind a tap, and on this project the recurring failure has been controls people could not find.
